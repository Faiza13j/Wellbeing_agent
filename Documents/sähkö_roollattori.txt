Sähkörullattori (electric rollator)

A sähkörullattori is an electrically assisted mobility aid designed to help people who have difficulty walking. 
It can be useful for older adults and people with disabilities who want to move around more independently, 
conserve energy and take part in everyday activities.

One important distinction: a traditional rollator provides support while the person walks, whereas an electric rollator may 
provide powered assistance or combine walking support with a wheelchair function, depending on the model.
How is it useful?

1. Supports walking

Provides hand support and stability for people who feel unsteady or have reduced walking endurance. Powered assistance, 
when included, can reduce the effort needed to move.

2. Helps manage fatigue

A model with a seat lets the user rest when tired. This can make short trips, shopping and outdoor activities more manageable.

3. Encourages independence

Can help the user move around the home, visit shops or meet friends with less reliance on another person, when the device suits
 their needs.

4. May combine walking and powered mobility

Some combination models can be converted from a rollator into an electric wheelchair. This can help someone who can walk short 
distances but needs powered mobility for longer journeys.

A sähkörollaattori is an electric rollator that helps a person move more easily and safely.

It can be used both indoors and outdoors, depending on the model and environment. It can help with walking in the home, care 
facilities, pavements and other suitable outdoor areas.

 sähkörollaattori models may provide motor assistance, adjustable speed, brakes, a seat and storage space.
 Who might benefit from it?

Older adults who tire quickly while walking.

People with reduced mobility due to a disability or long-term condition.

People who can stand and walk for short periods but need to sit for longer journeys.

People who want more independence when visiting shops, parks or other places.

However, a powered rollator is not suitable for everyone. A person who has severe balance problems, cannot safely stand, or cannot 
control the device may need a different mobility aid. A physiotherapist or occupational therapist can assess suitability.

How can someone get one in Finland?

Before purchasing such an expensive device, contact the local healthcare centre or assistive-device service. In Finland, basic 
rollators and more advanced powered mobility aids have different assessment and provision routes. Depending on the person's needs 
and eligibility, a powered wheelchair or other mobility aid may be provided through public healthcare.
